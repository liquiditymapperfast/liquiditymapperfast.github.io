# Layouts and the places of the panes (2026-10-31)

Branch `feat/personal-layouts` (from origin/master 682c17e), worktree `.tmp/layouts`. Page-only: nothing under `src/server`, `src/shared` or `tools` changed, so a ship is a push and `npm run build:site` in the main checkout, with no restart of the 8787 server.

## What was asked

"Make the website more personalizable ... I don't know how to close the two columns on the sides ... everything should be easy and intuitive ... drag windows and make their own layout, save it, load pre-existing ones ... a collection of really deeply thought through layouts as defaults ... open or position columns however I want."

## What there was

- `layout.ts`: a fixed grid, flow column | map and its panes | order book. Only the panes under the map could be reordered, by a small grip, and only among themselves. Nothing could be closed from the pane itself: the only switch was the Panels deck in the top bar, which is hidden when the bar is folded.
- A Layouts panel behind a small button in the status bar (not reachable at all on a touch tablet's compact bar): save, apply, update, delete, export, import. No ready-made layouts. Its "Default layout" reset every chart setting as well, because `settingsToApply` filled any field a layout did not carry with the page's default.

## What was decided, and why

**The model.** The map and the panes that share its time axis (Bar stats, Depth, Open interest, Delta, Long/Short, the Liquidity Tracker, Scripts) draw in the map's x coordinates: they stay in the map's column and can be moved among each other (not beside the map, not above it). The two panes with an axis of their own, the flow column and the order book, are *side panes*: each stands on either side of the map, alone or one over the other. Whether a pane is open stays `state.show`; where it stands is a `DockPlan` (`layouts/dock-model.ts`, pure and tested). A closed pane keeps its place; a column with nothing open is a 22 px strip with an edge tab for each pane it holds (30 px on a touch screen); a column with no pane in it is not there. The saved shape keeps the fields the first version wrote and adds `dock`, so an older page reads what a newer one wrote.

**Affordances, one for each way of asking.** × on every pane (not the Scripts pane: its switch is the Scripts feature, which draws on the map too); an edge tab to open a closed side pane; a grip, and for a side pane a title bar, to drag by (the whole header where a pane under the map holds no control); a drop mark (a bar between two panes, or the strip along the window's edge where a column is not there); the ⋯ menu and a right click for a keyboard and a finger (to the other side, up, down, swap the two sides, reset the size); arrow keys on every splitter; `[` and `]` for the two columns; Undo in a toast after applying a layout, hiding a pane, deleting a layout.

**Why a title bar for the side panes.** The first version put the buttons at the end of each header. Measured against the build before (`heads.mjs`, headless Chrome, 7 window widths), the order book's header went from 88 px (three rows) to 119 px (four) at the two narrowest column widths, 350 and 359 px: its first row is full to the last pixel there (the slack is about 10 px). Every attempt to keep the buttons in the header (a smaller reserve, a narrower gap, no grip) either still wrapped at 350 px or squeezed gaps to 4 px. A bar of its own above the header costs 26 px of a column that is 700 to 1,300 px tall and gives a bigger target to drag by; with it the header is 88 and 57 px at every width, as before. The panes under the map keep the buttons in their header (one row, room to spare; the only change measured is the Delta header wrapping at 1,280 px with both columns open, which it already did at 1,024).

**Where Layouts lives.** One button, in the map's corner (where a person looking at the map finds it), and, on a touch tablet's compact bar, a section of Settings. The status bar's button is gone: the same control in two places is what the Range button was before it left the deck. *Karl's call.*

**What a layout keeps.** By default the panes: which are open, where they stand, how big they are. "With the chart's settings" keeps everything the chart shows as before (what every layout saved by an earlier version did). Applying lays what a layout names over the chart as it is (`settingsToApply`), so a layout that names only the pane switches leaves the timeframe, the colours and the rest alone. (The advisor caught that a preset applied through the old path would have reset every setting to its default; `readLayouts` had the same fault for a partial layout, so it keeps only the fields a layout names, `carriedSettings`.)

## The ready-made layouts

One way of working each, as a function of the window (`presets.ts`): heights of the panes under the map are shares of the map's column, widths of the columns shares of the window, and a layout opens as many of its panes as fit with the map at its own minimum. They set the pane switches and the few chart switches the way of working is about (the footprint, for the order flow), nothing else.

| Layout | For | Open |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Default | How the page first opens (replaces the old "Default layout" button) | flow column from 1,500 px, order book |
| Map only | The picture alone: reading structure, screenshots, a small window | nothing |
| Order book | Where liquidity rests and whether it is pulled | a wide book (27 % of the window), Depth and the Liquidity Tracker under the map |
| Order flow | Who is buying and selling at market, and how hard | a wide flow column (24 %), Delta and Bar stats, the footprint on |
| Positioning | Who holds what and how it changes | Open interest, Long/Short, Delta under a wide map; both columns closed |
| Everything | A large screen | both columns and Bar stats, Depth, OI, Delta, L/S, LT |

"Mirrored" is not one of them: it is a transform, so it is the action "Swap the two sides" (⋯ menu and the panel), which turns any layout over. "Compact" is not one either: every layout is already a function of the window.

## Review round (2026-10-31)

A read-only reviewer ran the code against random inputs and a stubbed page (11 findings, each checked against the source before it was acted on; the ones that touch the screen were re-run in headless Chrome):

- **Duplicate hung the page** for a name of 34 to 40 characters: "Name copy 2" was cut back to a name that is taken, for ever. `copyName` cuts the name, not the ending, and gives up after the layouts a person can have.
- **A column with one open pane was never clamped** (a width kept in a wider window, or carried across by a move, could take the whole window). `laidOutWidths` holds every open column between what its panes need and 60 % of the window; the default width of a column is the book's whenever the book is open in it, whichever pane stands first (`leadOf`).
- **Undo** put back only the panes and switches, and put back everything done in the next nine seconds. It now puts back what the action changed, if it still stands as the action left it (`undoPatch`, `samePanes`), settings of a saved layout included.
- **A "panes only" save** carried every switch (the footprint, the Volume profile). It carries the switches that open a pane (`PANE_SWITCHES`), and a layout names only what it carries (`carriedSettings`, `settingsToApply`).
- **A phone** got the desktop's stack weights as an inline style (it beats the stylesheet); a window crossing into a phone is laid out again; moving a strip pane is refused on a phone.
- **Undoing a delete** at 20 layouts, or beside a layout that took the name, silently removed another one; it now says it cannot.
- **A date** in an imported file past year 3000 made the panel throw in every language but English; it becomes "no date".
- **Two CSS rules** with the same selector made the later one win (`.toast`, `.layout-name`, which is also the Scripts panel's input): one `.toast`, and the card's title is `.layout-title`.
- **A column closed with its key** reopened every pane it holds, including one shut on purpose; it reopens the ones it had open.
- **Renaming** a layout and then clicking another card replaced the card under the pointer, so the click was lost: the panel is redrawn once the click has been handled.
- **Accepted:** `[` and `]` are matched by their place on the keyboard, so on a German keyboard Ü flips the left column and the right column's key is the zoom's `+`. That is how the keys were chosen (where a bracket needs AltGr there is no key to press otherwise); the edge tabs and the Layouts panel do the same.

## Second review round (2026-10-31, before it went public)

Two read-only reviewers (Codex CLI `gpt-6.1-sol` at maximum effort, and a Sonnet agent at maximum effort) read the whole branch; every finding was checked against the source, and the ones that touch the screen were re-run in headless Chrome on a production build (`vite build`, `vite preview`).

- **Undo wrote a layout's VWAP and Volume profile settings back without the chart's own anchors and zone** (a snapshot leaves them out, so the patch said they were missing): with VWAP on, Undo of a saved layout that changed its bands left `vwap.anchors` undefined, and the chart threw on its next draw. `completeSettings` lays this chart's own back.
- **Two columns could still take more than their share.** `fitColumns` cut a column to its minimum and left the other cut by proportion alone: 200 + 560 px in a window of 1,024 left the map 252 px. The column that is not at its minimum now gives the rest.
- **A Scripts pane that stays open was not counted** when a ready-made layout chose how many panes fit under the map (`PresetEnv.held`): *Everything* opened five panes in 584 px that need 636.
- **Resizing two stacked side panes never marked the layout "changed since"**: the split between them is part of what the eye takes from the screen now (`sameShot`).
- **The splitters between the panes under the map had no double click** though the Guide said every splitter has one: a double click gives the panes it parts their own height.
- **Copy and Delete with storage that refuses** said "At most 20 layouts" and nothing; they now say the browser did not keep it. The Undo of a delete redraws the Layouts panel that is open then, not the one the layout was deleted from.
- **Text that said more than the code does**: the phone's pinch is per axis (sideways time, up and down price), the Default layout has no flow column below 1,500 px, and *Order flow* also switches the footprint on (the Guide's sentence said a layout decides only which panes are open and how big). Reworded in all twelve languages.
- **Sol's second look at the fixes** found five more, all real: a Scripts pane that draws nothing in a row was counted as taking room (the layouts reserved it by its switch, not by what shows: `scriptRows`); the columns' minimums were applied after the fit, so 150 + 480 px passed unchanged and then became 200 + 480 in 1,024 (`fitColumns` raises each to its minimum first); a reset in a window too short for the panes saved their squeezed heights as their own (it keeps the heights they start with, so a taller window gives them back); a stacked pair imported at the weight limit was dragged to weights that a file reads back clamped, so the saved layout showed as changed at once (a split is always weights that add up to 2: `splitWeights`); and the focus after the tour has Settings to go to on a compact bar.
- **Accepted:** the Undo toast is not focused (it is a status, nine seconds long, and the same layout can be applied again from the panel), the splitters share one label and carry no value, a key held on a splitter writes the layout at the repeat rate (a few hundred bytes).

## Not done, on purpose

Floating windows, tab groups inside a column, panes above the map, Maximize, a customizable top bar (its fitting code is delicate and the decks are the page's own order). Each is its own piece of work; none is needed to close, open, move, size, save and load.

His own Chrome on a 4K screen, Firefox, a real iPad (Chrome's touch emulation only). `hlm-layout-v2` is read once and then left as it was: an older build opened afterwards would show the sizes as of the migration (nothing is lost).
