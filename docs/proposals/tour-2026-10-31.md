# The tour (2026-10-31)

## What was asked

A guided tour beside the Guide: quick where the Guide is long, with a button that is visible and a little bigger than the rest, a considered answer to whether the first visit should ask for it, and the Guide kept (and improved: it may grow, it should not bloat). liquidate.fun was named as the model for the tour's design and for the first-visit question.

## What liquidate.fun does (read on 2026-10-31, in my words)

A banner offers the tour on a first visit, a button for it sits in the page's footer for good, and a welcome card tells where the replay is. The tour is seven spotlight steps with a card that glides from one to the next, dots and a counter, short copy; what it remembers is a small versioned record (version, when, skipped, finished). It never starts by itself. That is the shape taken here; what is different is below.

## What was decided, and why

**Never start by itself, ask once.** Three first-visit scenarios were weighed. *Start at once*: a modal in the first second, over a map that has nothing in it yet (it fills in as you watch), for people who came for something else; rejected. *A button only*: quiet, but a newcomer who never looks at the top right never learns there is one. *Ask once, without blocking* (taken): a notice under the top bar, a moment after the page has drawn ("New here? A one-minute tour shows what everything is for." with Take the tour and Not now); the page stays usable under it. It is the bar's own notice (`.notice`, as for a venue that is blocked), so it takes no room of its own and moves nothing; a third notice stacks under two others. Declining says where the tour is (a toast), and the question is never asked again, whatever the answer: the record says it was shown. Everyone without a record is asked once, regular visitors too (a notice is an announcement of something new).

**Modal.** A `<dialog>` as big as the window, opened with `showModal()`: the page under it is inert (no shortcut, no wheel, no click reaches the chart), Esc and the focus behave as in the Guide, and the page's shortcut handler already stands down for an open dialog. The light is a hole in a dim layer (a box shadow with a huge spread around a rectangle) that glides to the next step; under `prefers-reduced-motion` it does not. Tab goes round the card's buttons. The cost of modal is that a step cannot ask for a try ("scroll here"): the steps say what a thing is and the Guide's moving pictures show it.

**A step is data, and where it points is looked up when it is shown.** `steps.ts` (what it points at by name, the side its card prefers, its words; one list for a desktop, one for a touch tablet that keeps the desktop's panes, one for a phone), `targets.ts` (each name is a list of selectors, the first on the screen standing for it: a closed side pane is lit as its edge tab and the step then says how to open it; a step whose target is not on the screen is left out), `place.ts` (pure: the side that has room, beside what is lit, never off the window, inside a target as big as the window; 3,000 random windows in a test), `record.ts` (pure: what is read from storage and when to ask), `tour.ts` (the modal), `invite.ts` (the notice). A test holds every title to 30 characters and every body to 200: a tour is read, not skimmed past. The tour changes nothing on the page: a top bar that was folded is unfolded for it and folded again, an open panel or the Guide is closed first, and a window that becomes a phone ends it (the page is another page then).

**The steps.** Nine on a desktop (the map; looking around; coin, venues and timeframe lit together; panels; the layers of the chart; the flow column; the order book; layouts; the Guide), six on a touch tablet, four on a phone, which has tabs. The map's step says that a new page fills in as you watch, because the first thing a newcomer sees is a map with nothing in it. About a minute.

**The Guide.** Kept, as the long way, and improved: a section "Make it yours" (close, move, resize, the ready-made layouts, saving, Undo, the bracket keys), the Tour and Author named beside the Guide, the stale "eight themes" corrected, the start of the Guide points at the tour, and a Take the tour button in its title bar. It has no upper length (the header of `guide/content.ts` says what to add and what not to: nothing to fill a section, never a second way of saying what is said). Its button no longer calls itself a tour.

**The button.** The page's one filled button, in the page's own deck at the top right: the Tour and the Guide side by side on its first row, Author and the two marks (screenshot, installing) on the second. The deck is as wide as before in English; the bar's fit was measured at 1,280 to 1,920 px in four languages and four frames before and after. The first arrangement (the Tour and the Guide and the screenshot on the first row, Author under) made the deck 37 px wider in English and 75 to 100 in German and Russian: the bar zoomed lower in 23 of 96 cases (by up to 5 %) and broke into two rows in 11. On a touch screen the button is the first tile of Settings.

## Review (2026-10-31, before it went public)

Two read-only reviewers (Codex CLI `gpt-6.1-sol` at maximum effort, a Sonnet agent at maximum effort) read the branch; each finding was checked in the source, and each fix has a unit test or a headless-Chrome check on a production build (`tests/app-heat-zoom.test.mts`, the browser probes under `Research_Results/tour_20261031/`).

- **Starting the tour sent a synthetic Esc to the whole page**, which put an armed drawing tool away and unpinned the Bar stats. Panels, menus, the sheet and the Guide are closed by their own doors now; the tour's own Esc goes no further than the tour.
- **A start that failed** (`showModal` refusing, or a later step throwing) left its timer, its listeners and an inert dialog behind, and the next start's Esc could end the wrong tour. Everything is created once the dialog is up and taken down by `dispose`, once.
- **The Guide opened over the tour** when its words were still loading, and the Guide's "Take the tour" started it without unfolding a folded bar (four steps vanished) or putting the invitation away: the Guide's link takes the toolbar's own way in (`takeTour`).
- **The invitation was recorded as seen behind the Settings sheet** (a `div`, not a `dialog`); it waits while anything is open over the page, asks again for a minute, and records only when it is on the screen. **The notices** on a phone lay one under the other at fixed steps and covered each other's words (a blocked venue's notice is three lines): each is put under the one above by what that measures.
- **The wheel on a chart framed wider than the price limit zoomed in on a turn out**, then stood still (`limitFactor` drove a span back into range): a limit now stops only the turn that would go past it. It had the same fault, unseen, on the price scale and its drag.
- **A tab closed in the middle of a tour** that had unfolded a folded bar left it unfolded for good. **The focus after the tour** goes back to what began it; where that is gone it goes to the Tour button, to Settings on a compact bar (the Tour button is in the sheet there), or to the arrow that unfolds a bar the tour folded again (the toolbar moves the focus there whenever the control that held it goes with the fold, which also holds for the Hide button's own click).
- **Text that said more than the code does**: the pinch on a phone zooms one axis by the direction it moves (the tour said "together").

## After the ship (2026-11-01)

- **The two filled buttons of the tour lost their fill on Glass** (the Waves lamp; `data-material="glass"`), which none of the tests had switched on. Karl's pictures: in Terminal the invitation's "Take the tour" was near-black on near-black, in Light the bar's Tour white on a pale face. The one rule that gives a control a see-through face (`chrome.css`, specificity 0,7,2) beat the buttons' own fill (0,3,1 and 0,4,1), so the accent's label (`--on-ui`) sat on the glass face: contrast 1.0 to 1.8 against 4.5 or more solid, in 286 of the 299 theme and panel combinations measured. The rule now leaves `.tour-btn` and `.tour-invite-go` alone, and `tests/app-glass-fills.test.mts` fails when a button the style sheets fill with the accent is neither lit (`.on`), outside the bars and panels, nor named there.
- **Two older faults of the same kind** turned up in the audit (every control of every theme, solid against glass, at rest and hovered, pressed and focused): the Install mark's hover fill (its label is the page colour on the accent), and, in the three text-mode themes, every button's label while it is pressed (inverse video: the fill was taken, the label stayed the page colour). The rule leaves both alone now (`:not(.install:hover)`, `:not(:active)`).
- **Left as it is:** the lamp tint of an open panel's button (`button.open`) is still flattened on glass; its label reads (7.3:1 at the worst) and the accent outline stays.

## Not done, on purpose

A try-it step (it needs a page that is not inert); a step for the alerts deck, the drawing tools or the scripts (the Guide has them); starting the tour from a shortcut key; telling the tour apart for people who came back after months. Each is its own piece of work.

## Not verified

Firefox and Safari (`dialog.showModal`, `color-mix`, `clip-path` are all in their current versions), a real phone, a screen reader.
