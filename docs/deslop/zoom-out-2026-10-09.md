# Zoomed out over days: the flow column's span, and what made the page slow (2026-10-09)

Two reports: with the map zoomed out over several days and the flow column on **Map**, the column showed only about a fifth of the map's
time; and the page grew slow zoomed out. Measured on a read-only copy of a live recording (109 h of depth, 73 h of flow, 28 markets), a
private server on a spare port, headless Chrome at 2560 x 1313 CSS px (DPR 1.5), a real GPU path. The GPU was shared with an unrelated heavy
workload while these were taken, so only the CPU side (the page's main thread, the raster worker, the server) is reported as measured.

## The flow column's span

Not a drawing bug: three caps. The page loaded at most 24 h of flow (`HISTORY_CAP_MS`), its ring holds 2^17 seconds, and the server's
recorder keeps 36 h of seconds in memory, while SQLite keeps a week of minutes. The column trims its window to where the loaded flow begins
and said "Flow recorded since" at that point, so a 109 h map drew 24 h (0.22 of it).

Now a window older than the seconds the page holds is drawn from **minutes** there (`/api/v2/flow-minutes`, `FlowRecorder.minutes`): per
minute the USD bought and sold, the last price, and the lowest and highest the running delta reached in it, 20 bytes a minute. The server
answers from memory and, for what memory no longer holds, from the store. The page joins them (`cvd/track.ts`): the minutes are shifted to
meet the seconds at the first whole minute the seconds hold, so the line runs on without a step (a test adds both up from the same trades and
checks every minute end and every second after the join). Ranking, the quiet flag and the dot rows still count seconds. A window inside the
seconds reads only the seconds, as before. A source without minutes (the browser engine keeps a day; an older server) keeps the old window
and says why ("older flow is not available from this source").

The minutes older than the server holds in memory are summed once, at start, from the store (a week of rows read in order, about 0.8 s for 73 h of 14 markets) and as minutes leave memory: answering 110 h of 14 markets takes 40-50 ms. Reading them from SQLite on each request took 0.5 s, because one market's rows lie scattered through the file. The page asks again only when the window reaches further back or the seconds it holds begin later, never on the clock.

Checked end to end: a 60 h map's column now starts within a pixel of the map's start (it started 24 h back); a 110 h map's starts where flow
was first recorded (Oct 6 13:35), with that note.

## The server's column answer (the biggest cost)

Zooming out asks `/api/v2/columns` for twice the view at about two pixels a column; the recorder merged every minute of every market for it
with a `Map` per window and per bin. On the copied recording (28 markets, `DepthRecorder.query` + `encodeColumns`, Node, three runs each;
before, all three were the same cold merge):

| span (step) | before (best of 3) | after, first time | after, asked again |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 h (1 min, no merge) | 45 ms | 39-53 ms | the same |
| 24 h (2 min) | 4.5 s | 0.75-0.98 s | 0.14-0.16 s |
| 48 h (4 min) | 5.6 s | 0.9-1.2 s | 0.12-0.16 s |
| 110 h (8 min) | 5.3 s | 0.9-1.6 s | 0.13-0.15 s |

Over HTTP on a live server the merge blocked the event loop for 12-15 s at a time, so every other request (state, candles, prints, flow)
waited behind it. What changed (`shared/recorder.ts`):

- the window is found by binary search, not by copying and filtering every minute;
- windows are merged in flat arrays with the same sums in the same order as the map did (a test compares the two to the last bit at many
  steps and starts, ties included);
- `finish` (dropping the smallest bins of a large column) uses plain loops; it had used typed-array `from` with mappers and a full
  comparator sort, and was most of what was left;
- a merged window that has ended and lies wholly inside the question is kept (6 M bins, at most about 72 MB) and dropped at a prune;
- the page asks for steps that double (1, 2, 4, 8 ... minutes), so zooming moves between a few steps the server has merged before.

Fixed on the way: `.map(finalize)` passed the array index as `minBinUsd`, so merged windows never dropped bins under the floor.

Still large: an answer is 20-45 MB at these spans (every bin of the grid). Sending fewer bins for a coarse view would change what is sent
and what the cells and the depth pane are made from, so it is left as the next step.

## The page

Hovering a 110 h map, 80 pointer moves, all features on (CPU profile and trace of the same build, before and after):

- the page's main thread (trace): 82 % busy before, 47 % after; frames 50 ms at the median (20 fps) before, 16.7 ms (33 ms at the 90th
  percentile) after;
- the raster worker (trace): 66 % busy before, 20 % after;
- a zoom out from 2 h to 140 h: 90th-percentile frame 33 ms and 13 long tasks before; 17 ms and none after.

What changed:

- **Absorption** judged every recorded group (22,000 at 110 h) and grouped the marks into squares by comparing each with every square, on
  every frame: about 20 ms a frame. The judged marks are now kept until the groups or the thresholds change (`AbsorptionBook.marks`), the
  squares are grouped through a grid of 12 px cells with the same result (`iconsOf`, tested against the old way), and the squares are worked
  out again only when the map has moved by a whole pixel (drawn shifted by what it moved since).
- **The depth pane** asked the worker for a new series whenever the last one came back, because its key held the view to the millisecond and
  a map following the live edge moves every frame. It now counts the view in whole columns of its answer and refreshes what is live every 1.5 s
  (or three times the time an answer took). Its Highlights flags (a scan over many columns per column) are worked out once per answer.
- **The heatmap raster** was redone every 1.5 s whatever it cost; it is now redone no more often than three times its own duration.
- **Whale bubbles** had a 10 px blurred shadow each, every frame; the glow is drawn once per colour and size and copied. Taking the blur away
  halved the graphics process's work on the bubbles in a trace, but that trace shared the GPU with other work, so it is not a measurement.
